What you are doing, and why

TechCorp's incident response runbook is a draft, three days old, and nobody has walked it against the system it is meant to protect. You are the reviewer. You read the worked example TC-IR-001, walk Runbook v0.1 step by step against your own portal, and write the team lead a memo: adopt as written, adopt with changes, or do not adopt.

It practices the incident-response vocabulary of lecture section 10 and the method from the lab: extract the claim, test it against what exists, record a verdict with evidence. Plan about an hour.

The scenario

In the lab you handed the team lead a backlog with one priority 1 finding, a stopgap holding it, and a list of findings underneath. The lead's reply: "Fine. Now tell me what we do if one of these has already been used."

One of them has. TC-IR-001 is the completed report on an incident from the week before the term: an unauthenticated actor downloaded the portal's database, customer records and staff accounts, through the exposed data directory. Production was remediated before the term began. Your environment is the portal as it stood before that fix, which is why the lab found the door still open.

Two documents came out of that incident. Runbook v0.1 is corrective action CA-1, drafted by the lead from the carrier's playbook. Template TC-IR-01 is corrective action CA-2, the blank incident report form. The lead's circulation note asks for one thing: walk every step and mark anything you could not execute as written at 2 a.m. with the rest of the team unreachable.

What you are given

  • TC-IR-001, the completed report, on the TC-IR-01 Form and Worked Example page in the Course Orientation module. Its section 7 is the standard for an annotated confirmation.
  • Runbook v0.1 DRAFT, on the Incident Response Runbook page in this module. The document under review. Cite it by section and step number.
  • Template TC-IR-01, the blank form, on the same TC-IR-01 Form and Worked Example page. Its ten sections are named there, starting with "1. Administrative details".
  • Your lab artifacts: the claim register, the threat register, the findings backlog, and the stopgap you applied and labelled.
  • Lecture section 10, which defines every term the runbook uses: event, security incident, Discovery, the carrier and its Breach Response Unit, playbook and runbook, the Incident Commander and the IC log, the holding statement, severity tiers, the four lifecycle phases, mitigation and remediation, evidence preservation, and what OWASP A09 Security Logging and Alerting Failures (opens in a new tab) names.

Part 1 - The reading proof

  1. Read TC-IR-001, all of it, before you open the runbook. Then answer three questions, one or two sentences each, in the words of lecture section 10.
    • In section 7, why does Check 2 prove the remediation when Check 1 proves only the mitigation?
    • Why were credentials rotated even though the files were relocated? Which half of the incident does rotation close?
    • Why does section 5.3 exist, and where does each of its entries reappear in the report?

Part 2 - The review

  1. Walk the runbook the way you walked the design document. A runbook is a design document for the worst day. Produce at least eight findings, each with the fields below.

  • Test the concrete steps. Step 3 asks for a preservation copy of the application directory and data/portal.sqlite: take one in your Cloud Shell terminal the way lecture section 10 shows, and record the command, the hash, and who on the roster could do this at 2 a.m.
  • C-2 says to clear the server's session store. Open auth.php lines 18 to 23 and say whether the runbook tells the person at the keyboard where this portal's sessions live or how to clear them. Lecture section 10 says what a runbook owes that person that a playbook does not.
  • Section 5 starts the runbook when "the deny rule in front of the data directory records repeated refused requests". Look at the rule you added in the lab and say whether that trigger can fire on your portal.
  • Is there a finding in your backlog that fits two severity tiers in section 4, or none? Who is the IC when the lead is on a plane and the developer is on vacation?
  • At least three findings must be verified against your own environment or your own backlog, not reasoned from the armchair.

Open notes and open book are both fine. Respond in your own words; do not copy from other sources.

AI use on this assignment. The syllabus policy governs; these are its terms for a written review.

✓Permitted, with disclosure

  • Asking an AI to explain a concept, an OWASP guideline, or a PHP error message in general terms.
  • A grammar or spelling checker on your own writing.
  • Generating or refactoring code, proposing tests, and planning or editing the report, with disclosure of the tool, help requested, material used or changed, and checks performed. Report only results you observed.

✕Not permitted

  • Submitting AI-generated code, analysis, or writing without disclosing it.
  • Pointing an AI-assisted tool at any target other than your own course environment.

You must be able to explain every line you submit, on request. Undisclosed AI use is handled as academic dishonesty under the syllabus.

Expected output

A complete review has all seven parts in order: the reading proof, the findings walk, what the draft gets right, the section 10 extension, the severity drill, the template review, and the adoption memo. It is specific to this portal and this team throughout. A reader should be unable to lift your findings and apply them to another company's runbook without editing every one.

Two failure modes to check before you submit. The first is reviewing incident response in general instead of this runbook: if your findings never cite a step number, never name the database file, the session, or the three-person roster, you have written an essay. The second is the rubber stamp: a review whose verdicts are all "executable as written" has not walked anything.

What to submit

Submit your review as a Markdown (.md) file in your reports/ folder, written as the Writing in Markdown page in the Course Orientation module shows, part by part in the order of the instructions. Put the preservation-copy command and its output in a fenced block. Where you rely on a phase definition or a term, cite lecture section 10; where a limit is a recording gap, name OWASP A09.

At the top of the report, include this block, completed:

NAME: First Last

REFLECTION: Two or three sentences: what you decided, what was
difficult, and what you learned.
