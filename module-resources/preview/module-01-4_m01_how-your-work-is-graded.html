Four rubrics cover the graded work this term. Learn them once in the first week and you will know, for every assignment in every module, what full credit requires.

How every rubric works

  • Each rubric has five criteria worth 20 points each, 100 in total. Each criterion is scored at one of four levels: None (0), Partial 1 (10), Partial 2 (15), and Full (20).
  • The complete leveled rubric is attached to each assignment in Canvas, so you can read it before you start and see your scores against it after you submit.

Your final grade is weighted by category, not by raw points, so a forum post and an incident report contribute according to the weights on the Syllabus page.

A vulnerability module produces one submission with two parts. The report and the fix are graded together on one rubric: criterion 2 is the fix, and criteria 1, 3, 4, and 5 are the report.

Incident Report rubric

This rubric grades the report you write on the TC-IR-01 form and the fix it documents.

Incident Report rubric. Columns: Criterion, What full credit requires.
CriterionWhat full credit requires
1. Report completenessEvery required TC-IR-01 section is present, accurate, and scoped to this incident.
2. Verified fix ("prove it")The fix is correct, and section 7 shows the hostile request failing in your own environment after the change while legitimate use still works.
3. Principle and mitigation namedYou name the secure-coding principle and the mitigation technique precisely, and tie them to the correct OWASP control by name.
4. JustificationYou explain why the principle applies to this finding, in your own words, against your own evidence. Naming a principle without applying it never earns full credit.
5. Clarity and professionalismIt reads for someone who will not open the code; evidence is labeled and legible, and sources are cited.

Criterion 4 is where naming stops being enough. "Use prepared statements" is a name. Explaining why this query needed one, against your own trace, is the standard.

Hands-On Evidence rubric

This rubric grades the Module 01 setup lab report and the Module 02 lab evidence packet.

Hands-On Evidence rubric. Columns: Criterion, What full credit requires.
CriterionWhat full credit requires
1. Task completedEvery required step is done as specified.
2. Tool or environment used correctlyA correct, working setup that the rest of the course can build on.
3. Evidence is genuine and specificClear evidence tied unmistakably to your own environment and your own steps.
4. Self-verificationYou run the stated check and show the expected result.
5. Clarity and completenessWell organized and readable, with the NAME, AI DISCLOSURE, and REFLECTION block complete.

Criterion 4 is the one people lose points on. "I made the change" is not the standard. The standard is that you ran the stated check and showed the result.

Forum rubric

This rubric grades the seven case-study forums, each about one real incident whose root cause is the module's topic.

Forum rubric. Columns: Criterion, What full credit requires.
CriterionWhat full credit requires
1. Diagnose: what went wrongYou trace the failure in the module's vocabulary: the mechanism, not the headline.
2. Prevent: name the controlYou name a specific control at a specific point and say why it stops this attack.
3. Specific to this incidentYou name this incident's source, trust boundary, sink, and interpreter, as the lecture defines them.
4. Peer engagementYour reply is substantive: it stress-tests a classmate's control, or adds one at a different point in the chain.
5. Timeliness and communicationOn time, clear, professional, and respectful.

Criteria 1 and 2 are separate on purpose. Naming the failure earns credit on its own; the prevention argument is what the forum is assessing.

Design Project rubric

The Design Project rubric is a page in Module 08 and is attached to the project in Canvas. It grades the team's design document on four criteria and each member's own work on the fifth:

  1. Threat model and requirements. Grounded in the requirements memo and the term's incidents: actors, what they may do, and the risks the design must answer.
  2. Safeguard specificity. Each safeguard names the control, the exact point it is enforced, the threat it prevents, and the curl evidence that verifies it.
  3. Cross-section coherence. Authentication, authorization and data, and operations agree on shared controls and read as one design.
  4. Verification plan. For each safeguard, the hand-typed curl request that shows it works and the result it should return.
  5. Individual build and decision under challenge. Your required core runs with curl evidence, and you engage the strongest objection to a safeguard you authored, then defend it with reasoning or concede with a stated reason.

The project is one grading category, split three ways: the draft at Module 12 (5 points of the course grade), the team design document at Module 16 (5), and your individual build with its decision under challenge at Module 16 (10). Criteria 1 to 4 grade the team document; criterion 5 grades you alone. The attribution table, which member authored and built which part, is what makes the individual grade fair.

The midterm

The midterm in Module 08 has two parts. The timed practical is taken in Canvas without assistance: one seeded flaw from the Modules 01 to 07 material is placed in your own portal copy, and you diagnose it, repair it, prove the repair, and submit a Markdown report. The practical page in Module 08 states the conditions; Canvas states the time limit and the window. The written companion is a take-home analysis of one documented breach: the root cause with its CWE, the control that would have prevented it, and the response judged on detection, containment speed, and disclosure. The practical is 15 points of the course grade and the companion 5.

How to use these before you submit

  1. Open the rubric attached to the assignment in Canvas.
  2. Read the Full column for each criterion. That is the target.
  3. Check your draft against it, criterion by criterion.
  4. Fix the weakest one. It is worth as much as the strongest.

Where your feedback appears

I grade against these rubrics in Canvas and add comments and annotations to your submission. Feedback can appear in the rubric itself, in annotations on your file, or in the assignment comments, so check all three. If a comment is unclear, message me. Details are on the Communication, Interactions & Course Guidelines page.
