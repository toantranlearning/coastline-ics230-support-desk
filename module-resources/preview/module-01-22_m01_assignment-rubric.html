Incident Report Rubric

Grades every module's two-part deliverable: the incident report (written on the TC-IR-01 form) and the hands-on fix it documents. One submission, one grade, five criteria at 20 points each, 100 total.

Levels: None 0 · Partial 1 = 10 · Partial 2 = 15 · Full = 20.

The report and the fix are graded together on purpose. A fix with no defensible account is half the work, and an account with no working fix is the other half. The confirmation section (§7) is where the two meet: the exploit fails on the student's own instance after the change, and legitimate use still works.

What this rubric enforces

  • Criterion 3 carries the written SLO evidence. Describing the principle (SLO 1) and stating the mitigation technique (SLO 2) are graded here, in the report itself, not only on the quiz. The words "the principle" and "the mitigation technique" are deliberate; a report that fixes the bug without naming either has not met the outcome in writing.
  • Criterion 4 is the integrity control. Course material supplies compressed one-line statements of each principle, and students are expected to reuse them when explaining risk to others. Naming the correct principle is genuine retrieval and earns partial credit. It must never earn full credit on its own: full credit requires answering why does that apply to this finding? against the student's own incident evidence.
  • Criterion 2 keeps grading objective. The pass check is deterministic: the seeded flaw is reproduced before the fix and fails after it, in the student's own portal copy in Google Cloud Shell, with requests typed by hand. The §7 evidence is that before and after, annotated.

How to use this rubric

Read the criteria before starting, then check your submission against the full-credit descriptions. Only the sections and tests required by the assignment are assessed. Supplied case facts are background, not evidence that you performed a test. Explain required unknowns rather than inventing results.
