Why this page matters more in this field than in most

You are training for work where people hand you access to their systems and their customers' data because they trust you. That trust is the product. Every security certification and professional body treats integrity as a licensing condition, not a personality trait; the ISSA Code of Ethics (opens in a new tab) (opens in a new tab) requires members to "perform all professional activities and duties in accordance with all applicable laws and the highest ethical principles" and to "discharge professional responsibilities with diligence and honesty." The CISSP code is stricter still, and violating it costs people their certification and their career.

Academic integrity here is not a hoop. It is the first rep of the thing you are being hired to do, and the person who cannot explain their own fix gets found out in the first technical interview. That is a far more expensive place to find out than this course.

The policy

You are responsible for reading, understanding, and following the Coastline College Academic Honesty Policy (opens in a new tab) (opens in a new tab).

Academic dishonesty includes, but is not limited to:

  • Submitting another person's work (code, prose, a discussion post, a report) as your own.
  • Having another person complete assessed work for you, or using a service beyond the assistance authorized in this course.
  • Copying from a source without crediting it, or using exact words without quotation marks.
  • Taking an exam for someone else, or having someone take one for you.
  • Obtaining or sharing exam or quiz answers before, during, or after the assessment.
  • Using unauthorized materials or aids during an assessment.
  • Knowingly letting another student copy your work, or otherwise helping someone be dishonest.
  • Submitting work from another course without the instructor's permission.
  • Falsely claiming technical difficulty with an online quiz, assignment, or exam.
  • Interfering with another student's or the instructor's work.

Consequences follow the college policy and scale with the violation: a zero on the item, a failing course grade, and referral for disciplinary action. If you are not sure whether something is allowed, ask me first; that question has never once gotten a student in trouble.

Generative AI in this course

Generative AI assistance is permitted on labs, module assignments, forum preparation, and the design-project build. Students may request explanations, generate or refactor code, propose tests, compare designs, and revise drafts. This course develops secure-coding judgment on top of prior programming experience. Students are assessed on the accuracy of their reasoning, their verification, and their ability to explain the work they submit.

AI-assisted code may be submitted when the student has read it, checked its fit with the existing application, tested it, and can explain its security mechanism and limitations. Written reports and forum posts may use AI for planning and editing; the final claims must reflect the student's own analysis and observations. A tool's suggested result is not evidence that a test passed.

Students disclose the tool, the help requested, the output used or changed, and the checks performed in the AI DISCLOSURE line of the report, or a short note at the end of a forum post. Disclosed AI assistance is allowed and does not itself reduce the grade. Fabricated evidence, undisclosed assistance, or presenting unverified generated claims as observations does not meet the course requirements.

Quizzes, the midterm practical and its companion assessment, and the design project's individual decision under challenge remain unaided. AI assistance, including editor completions, is disabled during these assessments. Specific assessment instructions govern any exception.

Students use only demo data and their own authorized portal copy. They do not upload credentials, real personal data, classmates' submissions, or protected assessment content. AI instructions are a learning aid, not an enforcement mechanism. Students remain responsible for following the course policy.

A useful disclosure

AI DISCLOSURE: I used Gemini to propose a validation function and explain its pattern. I changed the accepted character set to match the assignment. I ran PHP syntax checks and tested an invalid input, a boundary value, and a legitimate name. The report describes the results I observed.

Citing sources

In this course you will lean on external references constantly, and that is encouraged. Citing them is what makes it scholarship instead of copying.

  • Quote exactly? Quotation marks plus the source.
  • Paraphrasing an idea? Still cite it, in your own sentence structure; reworded copying is still copying.
  • Using an OWASP Cheat Sheet fix? Name the cheat sheet. That is exactly what a professional does in a code review, and it strengthens your work.
  • Adapting code from the PHP Manual, an OWASP Cheat Sheet example, or Stack Overflow? Say so in a comment, and explain in your rationale why it is correct for your case.

The Purdue OWL guide to avoiding plagiarism (opens in a new tab) (opens in a new tab) is the clearest short reference if you want one.

Four habits that prevent accidental plagiarism

Most plagiarism I see is careless rather than dishonest: a paragraph pasted into notes in module two and forgotten by module five. These four habits prevent it:

  1. Track your sources as you research, not afterward.
  2. Paraphrase or quote deliberately, and add your own thinking alongside it.
  3. Credit the author in the text and in a reference list.
  4. Run a similarity check before you submit.

Free or built-in options for step 4: the Similarity Checker in Microsoft Word (opens in a new tab) (opens in a new tab) (a 3-minute video), Grammarly (opens in a new tab) (opens in a new tab), and the Indiana University plagiarism tutorials and tests (opens in a new tab) (opens in a new tab), which are genuinely good for calibrating what counts.

Integrity in this course extends past the gradebook. Testing a system you do not own, with anything, is not an academic matter. It is a conduct violation and a crime.

The complete rule is on the Working in Google Cloud Shell page. Read it before the first hands-on step, and ask me before you run anything against a target you are unsure about.

Supplied report text and examples

TechCorp’s supplied corporate narrative, policies, approvals, and EXAMPLE responses are authorized course material. Keep them in the Word report. Your contribution is the marked answers, actual observations, screenshots, reasoning, and Revision History. Keeping the supplied text is expected; presenting an EXAMPLE or a generated transcript as a test you performed is not. Disclose assistance in the report’s designated entry and use your own actual date and name in the revision row.
