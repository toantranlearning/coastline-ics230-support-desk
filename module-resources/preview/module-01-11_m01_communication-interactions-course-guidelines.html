Communication is the single most important variable in an online course. Students who write to me early almost always finish; students who go quiet almost never do.

How to reach me

Toan Tran, MS, CISSP Canvas Inbox: the primary way to reach me. Use it first, for everything. Email: tqtran@coastline.edu Office voicemail / text: 714-241-6189 Office hours: Monday–Thursday, 6:30–8:00 p.m. Pacific Time Appointments: book a Zoom slot at https://scheduler.zoom.us/toan-tran-xq4byl

Write to me through the Canvas Inbox. It keeps our whole conversation attached to this course, so when you write in Module 11 about something we discussed in Module 04, I can find it. It also forwards to your email and retains a record of the exchange, which is what the college requires for course correspondence.

Check your Canvas Inbox, your Coastline email, and Announcements every day. Not twice a week. Deadlines move, answers arrive, and a message you read on Tuesday is worth more than the same message read on Sunday.

I respond within 24–48 hours, sometimes longer on weekends, holidays, and breaks.

Use this subject line

Note. ICS C230 - Firstname Lastname C1234567 - short description

For example: ICS C230 - Tommy Bahamas C2346340 - search page returns a 500 error

It looks fussy, but I teach several sections and a subject line like that gets you a faster and more useful answer than "help." Use it in the Canvas Inbox and in email alike.

How feedback reaches you

I grade labs, written assignments, and discussions against a published rubric, and I add comments and annotations directly to your submissions. Feedback can appear in three places, so check all of them:

Discussion forums in this course

On odd modules, after the lecture, a forum presents a short case study of a recent, real incident caused by exactly the bug the module teaches. You post before the lab work, so that when you meet the vulnerability hands-on you already know what it costs in the world.

A full-credit post does three things:

  1. Diagnoses the failure: what the organization did wrong, traced in the module's own vocabulary, the mechanism rather than the headline.
  2. Names the prevention: the specific control, at the specific point, that would have stopped this exact attack, and why.
  3. Engages a classmate: reply substantively to at least one peer: stress-test their prevention, or add a control at a different point in the chain.

That "diagnose it and name the control" requirement is the heart of the course. Anyone can repeat a headline; the professional can say which line of code, which check, which policy was missing. The explanation is the skill, and it is what gets you the points.

Timing: post and reply to a classmate. I read every thread and post a summary of where the class landed each module.

Writing a reply worth reading

"I agree" and "good post" earn nothing. A reply that counts does at least one of these:

  • Connects the point to your own experience or your own lab result.
  • Says whether you agree and why, with a specific reason.
  • Brings in a reputable source: an OWASP Cheat Sheet, the PHP Manual, a documented breach.
  • Offers a different angle, with a concrete example.
  • Answers a classmate who asked for help. I monitor for misinformation and will step in when needed.

Netiquette

  • Be concise and specific. About one screen, with a subject line naming the topic.
  • Skip sarcasm. People who do not know you will read it wrong.
  • Proofread. Read the post aloud first; tone is most of online communication.
  • No all-caps, no flaming. Disagree with ideas, never with people.
  • Respect privacy. Do not forward or quote a private message without permission.

Course guidelines

First-week participation

I drop students who do not participate during the first week. To participate, do these before Sunday 11:59 p.m. of week one:

  1. Work through every page in the Course Orientation module and complete the Student Learning Contract.
  2. Post your Module 01 introduction and reply to at least one classmate.
  3. Submit the Module 01 setup evidence and take Quiz 1.

If any of this is going wrong (your environment will not launch, you registered late, your laptop died), message me before the deadline. I will work with you. Silence is the only thing I cannot work with.

Ongoing participation

I review participation throughout the term. If you have not logged in or submitted work in five days, I may drop you. Contact me as soon as a problem appears; a plan made in advance is almost always possible.

Dropping and withdrawing

You are responsible for officially dropping a course you no longer attend. Check your Student Class Program (Web Schedule Bill) in MyCCC (opens in a new tab) (opens in a new tab) for this course's refund, drop, and withdrawal deadlines; they vary by section. If you do not drop before the final withdrawal deadline, you will receive a grade for the course regardless of whether you completed it, which usually means an F on your permanent transcript. See Withdrawals on the support page.
