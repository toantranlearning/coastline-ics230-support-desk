One real incident whose root cause is this module's topic. Say what went wrong and how it could have been stopped, then engage a classmate. The deep analysis belongs in your incident report.

The incident

On Sunday, March 9, 2025, second-generation Chromecast and Chromecast Audio devices stopped working with Google's own apps. Owners saw an "Untrusted device" error and could not cast. Nobody attacked anything. Each device holds a certificate that chains up to a Google-owned intermediate certificate authority. Google's backend checks that chain to confirm the device is genuine. Users and independent researchers traced the outage to that intermediate. It had been valid for ten years, and it expired that afternoon. Once it lapsed, the dates check failed for every device at once. The backend refused them all, and millions of discontinued devices lost their main function on the same day. A security control did exactly its job, against its owner's own fleet.

The facts, from two independent reports and the certificate industry's own account:

  • Affected: Chromecast (2nd gen) and Chromecast Audio, both launched in 2015 and discontinued before the outage.
  • Cause, as traced by users and researchers: a Google-owned intermediate certificate authority that expired on March 9, 2025, at 16:44 UTC, after ten years of validity. Google's public statements described a problem and a fix. They did not name the certificate.
  • Google told owners not to factory reset. A reset device could not be set up again until a separate Google Home app update arrived, because the setup app enforced the same expired chain.
  • On March 13, Google said: "We've started rolling out a fix for the problem with Chromecast (2nd gen) and Chromecast Audio devices, which will be completed over the next few days." The Register reported that the fix moves the devices to a new Google-owned authority that expires in 2045. Google did not confirm that detail.
  • There is no CVE. Nothing was exploited and no data left anywhere.

Sources: The Register's report on the outage (opens in a new tab), The Register's report on the fix (opens in a new tab), Bitdefender's Hot for Security write-up (opens in a new tab), and DigiCert's post on the expiration (opens in a new tab).

Initial post

Write 150 to 250 words answering both questions about the Chromecast outage, in this module's vocabulary:

  1. What did they do wrong? Trace the failure through the machinery the lecture teaches: the certificate the device presented, the intermediate it chained to, the dates check the backend ran, and what a check that fails closed meant for a whole fleet at once. Say why "the certificate was valid when we shipped" was not enough for a product meant to run for ten years.
  2. How could it have been prevented? Name at least one specific control at a specific point in the certificate lifecycle, and say why it stops this exact outage rather than outages in general. Three are fair game: monitoring the expiry date of every certificate in the chain, with an alert well before the date; a renewal path for the intermediate, designed in before launch; and shorter lifetimes that force renewal to be automatic.

A strong post names the mechanism, not only the headline.

Peer reply

Reply directly to at least one classmate, in at least 75 words. Open with their name and sign yours. Stress-test their prevention: would their control have caught this before March 9, 2025, or only told someone the fleet was already down? Then add one control at a different point in the lifecycle, so the two of you have sketched defense in depth. Post early enough in the module for a real exchange.
