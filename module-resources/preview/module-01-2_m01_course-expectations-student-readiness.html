Reports are scaffolded

Download the Word template from Canvas and read its supplied corporate narrative. Keep the [STUDENT] prompts and EXAMPLE text; write your own answers after Answer: and paste your own screenshots into the report. Add your name, date, and contribution to Revision History at the end. Modules 1–2 give exact actions and ask for short responses; Module 3 asks you to make and explain a few choices. Later assignments expand the analysis as the technical work develops.

Word reports are edited in Word or Word Online, outside Cloud Shell. The IDE is for code, commands, and evidence collection. See Word Reports and Assignment Files for the complete submission steps.

How this course reads

The reading in this course is college level and stays there deliberately. Specifications, OWASP standards, vendor advisories, and CVE write-ups are written for practitioners, and nobody rewrites them into something friendlier once you are being paid to read them. Building that tolerance is part of what a transfer-level course is for.

This is not separate from the security content; it is the same skill. A requirement you misread produces software that is wrong, and wrong software damages a business every day it runs, which is a career problem before it is ever a security problem. Most vulnerabilities begin in the same place: something the specification failed to say, or something a developer read past. Precision in reading is what prevents both.

So technical vocabulary is defined once and then used. When a sentence resists you, reread it before you go looking for an easier source. That effort is the work, not an obstacle to it.

Time commitment

This is a 3-unit, 16-module course: 54 hours of lecture-equivalent work and 108 hours of outside work. Plan on roughly 10 hours per module; about 3½ hours of lecture and quiz, and about 6½ hours of lab work, coding, and writing.

Two or three sessions a week works far better than one long Sunday. The lab work is debugging-shaped: you will hit a wall, walk away, and see the answer twenty minutes later. That does not happen if Sunday night is all you have.

What I expect from you

  • Read the full syllabus in this module. It is the contract for the course.
  • Check your Canvas Inbox, your Coastline email, and Announcements daily. That is where course communication lives, and a message read on Tuesday is worth more than the same message read on Sunday.
  • Track your due dates on the Module Schedule page.
  • Participate in the module forum: post, and respond substantively to a classmate.
  • Submit your own work, thoughtfully completed and proofread.
  • Ask early. Reach out well before the due date, not the night of.
  • Test only your own course environment, never anyone else's. See the authorized-use rule on the materials page.

What you can expect from me

  • I communicate through the Canvas Inbox and Canvas Announcements; your Coastline email is the fallback.
  • I respond to email and voicemail within 24–48 hours (longer on weekends, holidays, and breaks).
  • I monitor every discussion and post a summary of the class's thinking at least once a week.
  • I return individual feedback on assignments within one week of the due date, through the rubric, submission annotations, or assignment comments.
  • I will work with you to make this a successful term. Tell me when something is not working.

Deadlines and late work

Quizzes and exams are single-attempt and close on the due date. Labs and written assignments are accepted up to 7 days late at 10% per day, and nothing is accepted after the last day of instruction. The full policy is on the Syllabus page.

If something serious comes up, contact me. Arrangements made in advance are possible; retroactive ones usually are not.

Falling behind in this course is unusually hard to recover from, because each module's lab builds on your own copy of the portal and the habits from the module before. If you slip, do not go quiet. Message me and we will pick a recovery order.

Four Canvas settings that save you grief

  1. Set your notification preferences (Account → Notifications) so announcements reach you by email or text. Course changes are announced there.
  2. Add a profile image (Account → Settings). It makes the discussions feel like a room with people in it.
  3. Use Modules as your entry point. It is the course in delivery order.
  4. Click Next at the bottom of each page to move through a module without missing an item.
