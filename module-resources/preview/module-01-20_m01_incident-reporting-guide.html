Incident Reporting From Finding to Verified Repair

On this page

  • Read the corporate record
  • Use prompts and examples correctly
  • Follow Read → Fix → Prove → Report
  • Separate facts from conclusions
  • Explain impact and likelihood
  • Preserve readable evidence
  • Understand the NIST connection
  • Complete Revision History
  • Review and submit

Your report connects a technical defect to a business decision: what happened, why it matters, what changed, and what the evidence supports. The supplied Word report models the structure and writing of a corporate report. It is substantially completed before you begin.

Read the corporate record

Download the .docx from the Canvas assignment and open it in Word or Word Online. Read the executive summary, company roles, case history, risk assessment, response plan, and management decisions. These are supplied case facts. Your assignment identifies the code change, tests, and student entries you must complete.

Modules 1–2 give exact actions and ask for brief responses. Module 3 adds a few implementation decisions and more explanation. A long supplied report does not mean a long writing assignment: complete the marked entries at the requested depth.

The full worked case provides another professional example. Its additional organizational actions are reading context, not tasks added to your assignment.

Use prompts and examples correctly

Every [STUDENT] request includes an EXAMPLE showing the expected kind and length of response. Keep the prompt and example, then enter your own answer after Answer:. Test-table entries and screenshot captions follow the same pattern.

EXAMPLE: “The working portal refused the database request with 403 Forbidden, while signed-in customer navigation still worked.” That is a model sentence. Write it as your own result only if your test actually demonstrated it.

Do not replace a failing result with an expected one. Explain the failure, any correction, and the final observed result. Leave supplied corporate approvals and history intact; record your own identity and actual collection time in the analyst entries.

Follow Read → Fix → Prove → Report

  1. Read: understand the assignment and supplied case; reproduce the starting behavior in the original portal on port 8080 and inspect its code.
  2. Fix: make the scoped change under fixed/, preserving earlier repairs. Run PHP syntax checks on changed PHP files.
  3. Prove: repeat the hostile check on port 8081 and test normal use. Compare with the unchanged original. State what each check proves and what it leaves unresolved.
  4. Report: add observations, repair reasoning, risk judgments, and screenshots to the Word report. Complete Revision History and submit all required parts to Canvas.

The setup module checks environment readiness rather than proving a security repair. Module 2’s deny rule is a mitigation for direct file access; moving sensitive storage outside the served directory is a separate design change. Module 3 repairs validation and rendering while keeping the SQL limitation visible.

Separate facts from conclusions

Separate facts from conclusions. Columns: Basis, How to use it.
BasisHow to use it
Supplied case factKeep the organization’s stated roles, history, decisions, and starting symptoms as supplied context.
Your observationRecord the input or command, copy and port, actual result, collector, and collection time.
EXAMPLEUse it to understand the expected answer; it is not a record of a test you ran.
InferenceExplain why the evidence supports it and what uncertainty remains.
Unresolved issueState the limitation and the follow-up needed; do not imply it was repaired.

A screenshot of bold text can establish HTML interpretation. It does not, by itself, establish script execution, stolen sessions, or a production breach. A 403 response for one route does not establish that all sensitive storage is protected.

Explain impact and likelihood

Read TechCorp’s initial assessment and likelihood–impact matrix. In your marked response, confirm or revise the impact and likelihood ratings and explain the business consequence. Connect the repair to reduced harm or reduced opportunity for exploitation. Keep your explanation within the requested length.

Impact describes the seriousness of possible harm. Likelihood considers reachability, required access, effort, and other prerequisites. The report’s matrix is TechCorp’s qualitative method, not a universal scoring rule. Module 1 assesses the reliability of the setup process rather than claiming a customer-data vulnerability.

Preserve readable evidence

Paste commands and output as selectable text. Identify the input, observed response, copy and port, and what the check demonstrates. Keep original captures and describe any redactions in submission copies. Do not attach downloaded customer databases or expose live credentials.

For each screenshot, paste your own image into the matching Word entry, add a concise caption, and provide descriptive alt text. Use In Line with Text and resize proportionally. A typed filename alone does not embed the picture.

The supplied corporate report describes responsibility for evidence handling and communications. Your course task is to collect the assigned evidence and submit it through Canvas. Do not contact case staff, customers, insurers, regulators, or other parties.

Understand the NIST connection

The report models incident-response practices using NIST SP 800-61 Revision 3 and CSF 2.0 as references: clear ownership and preparation, analysis and prioritization, recorded evidence, coordinated mitigation, recovery criteria, and improvement. The supplied organization’s roles, policies, and approvals let you see how a developer’s technical work fits those decisions. You do not have to invent an organization’s program to complete an entry.

Source: NIST SP 800-61 Revision 3 (opens in a new tab). TechCorp’s internal policy choices are case context, not universal NIST requirements.

Complete Revision History

At the bottom of the report, keep the initial TechCorp entry. Enter your actual revision date, your name, and a concise summary of your additions in the next row. Each requested field has an EXAMPLE. This identifies your contribution to the report; the complete changed source files separately record the implementation.

EXAMPLE — summary: “Added observed before-and-after statuses, repair verification, screenshots, and revised risk reasoning.”

Review and submit

Use Word Reports and Assignment Files for exact steps. Check the required student entries, actual results, limitations, AI disclosure, image captions and alt text, and Revision History. Keep the supplied title and heading styles, run Word’s accessibility checker, and save the completed .docx.

For a code-repair assignment, upload the Word report, unchanged supplied assignment.md, and every required complete changed source file together. Screenshots embedded in Word are included in that report. Add separate files only when directed or when referenced evidence is not embedded. Follow the assignment’s exact submission list; special assessments and projects may specify other formats.
